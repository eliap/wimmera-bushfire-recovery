Part 1

Where you are

What country is this, and how does it respond to fire?

After a fire, most people want to know one thing: was this a “good fire” or a “bad fire” for my block?

A fire can’t be classified quite so easily, but we can get a sense of how challenging recovery might be for sensitive species by learning how that country has adapted to burn. Part 1 will help you work out what sort of country you have: your bioregion and geology, your vegetation type, and when your place burnt before this.

This will tell you how resilient your block likely is to fire and whether it was at a stage where its plants and animals were ready for it. This can help you work out whether any species will need extra help as the habitat renews.

Start by looking up your block

Open the Wimmera Bushfire Ecology Lookup Tool, built as part of the Wimmera Landcare Fire Recovery Partnership. It brings together publicly available state data on your block’s bioregion and geology, vegetation type, fire history, fire intervals and growth stage, and lists the plants that belong in your bush.

Open the Wimmera Bushfire Ecology Lookup Tool →

Using the lookup tool

  1. Find your block. Type your address into the search box at the top, or zoom the map and click your block. The tool outlines the property title you picked in white, and names your catchment management authority and local Landcare group. It covers the Wimmera catchment only, the dashed line on the map.
  2. Add the rest of your place. If your property is more than one title, tick Add neighbouring titles and click the others. Clear selection starts again.
  3. Read down Part 1. The sections are numbered to match this guide, 1.1 to 1.5. As you scroll, the map shades your block for the section you are reading. Click a row in any table to highlight that part of your block in yellow; click it again to clear it.
  4. Write it down. At the end of Part 1 the tool gathers everything into a short record you can copy, save or print. Take it to your Landcare group, or keep it with your property plan (Part 5).

Very large titles, such as a national park, are looked at as a 150-metre circle around where you clicked, to keep the tool quick.

Once you have found your block in the lookup tool, follow along through Part 1 of this guide, where we explain what the information in the lookup tool means for you.

Follow along · the example block near Pomonal

Through Parts 1 and 2 we use the block the lookup tool opens on: 169 hectares of bush and cleared paddock on the edge of the Grampians near Pomonal, and one of the project’s nine demonstration properties. It burnt in the 2006 Mt Lubra fire and again, in part, in the 2025 Yarram Gap Road fire, so it shows most of what the tool can tell you.
